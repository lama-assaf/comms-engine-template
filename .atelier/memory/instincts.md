## structural instincts

- **single-pillar test (never skip)**: before any output, ask which *single*
  stream and which single theme it serves. "Both" means split into two
  outputs. "Neither" means kill it. This is the engine's central discipline.
- **proof discipline**: content without proof is noise. Every claim traces to a
  shipped component or to a row in `claims.md`. Product launches are ours to
  spin; a regulator, a standards body or a customer citing us is not, and that
  second kind is what the stream is scored on.
- **never claim ahead of shipping**: what has shipped is "done"; what is being
  built is "underway, with dates you can hold us to"; what is planned is
  "intended". Never "finished", "live" or "certified" for a layer that has not.
  The audience has a good nose for the claim-to-evidence gap.
- **specifies vs. implements**: say what the design does *and* what is running.
  A property in a specification never wears the present tense.
- **check `status.md` before any claim**: `project.md` is the strategy;
  `status.md` is what is evidenced. They diverge, and the gap is exactly where
  an unpublishable claim gets written.
- **a stat ships with its citation or it does not ship**: the ban was never on
  numbers; it was on unsourced ones. A figure carries its source and a link.
- **a pitch is not a partner**: a deck aimed at a party evidences intent, not a
  relationship. Every partner name needs a row in `handles.md` with its
  clearance, or the "in discussion, participation not confirmed" footer.
- **sober beats hype**: if a regulator would wince, rewrite it. Aim for the nod.
  Banned: "revolutionary", "game-changing", committee-speak, unverified stats.
- **contest without confrontation**: argue the structure, not personalities.
  Let regulators, standards bodies and customers carry the point by proxy. No
  gloating, no name-calling.
- **run a logic, don't assert a conclusion**: when a draft feels flat it is
  usually missing an argument structure, not an adjective. `logics.md` holds
  the shapes; the ones that need nothing shipped are the evergreen floor.
- **70 / 20 / 10 cadence**: 70% evergreen, 20% reactive, 10% milestone.
  Evergreen is the floor that keeps a stream warm between milestones.
- **one argument per week per theme**: if the calendar already carries a piece
  under a theme in the coming seven days, hold a new proposal under that theme
  to the week after. Read `calendar.md` before proposing anything.
- **platform-native, not copy-paste**: the same idea is re-cut per platform's
  register. Never ship identical text to three places; teasers are cut from the
  article, never copied, and no evergreen post repeats within a quarter.
- **website first**: the full post lives on the brand's site; LinkedIn and X
  carry teasers with one theme hashtag, cleared tags only and a CTA back to the
  post and nowhere else. A team post is bound by the same pipeline as an engine
  draft, and is added to the calendar before it goes out.
- **public copy carries no editorial voice and no stage directions**: a reader
  must not be able to tell the piece was gated. Sentences about how the piece
  was written, what the draft should claim or where the link goes belong in
  the frontmatter, never in the article or a teaser.
- **evidence chain**: every output sits on Ship, Benchmark, Certify, Publish,
  Cite, Compound. If it is not on the chain, it is not ready.
- **make proposed look proposed**: any roadmap, matrix or architecture picture
  we publish lets a reader see shipped against proposed without a disclaimer.
- **the latest dated document governs a conflict**: when two sources disagree
  on a fact, a status word or a role, read from the later-dated one and record
  both dates. The rule picks the document; it never clears a name, loosens an
  evidence grade or revives a retired date.
- **golden-hour plan**: never ship a thread or announcement without a staged
  first-hours engagement plan.
- **replies answer, they never argue**: answer a real question with the fact
  and its cleared hedge; contest a wrong claim once, with a primary source,
  then stop. Comments on others' posts add a fact or a sharper question, never
  a product mention or a link. Caps and dry-run state live in `streams.md` and
  `engine.json`; the record of replies is patterns only, never per person.
- **stealth is stealth**: nothing on the denylist in `handles.private.md` is
  mentioned in public, in any form, until a decision file clears it. The terms
  are named there and nowhere else in the repository; when in doubt, cut the line.
